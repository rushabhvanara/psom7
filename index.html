Learning Objective

Investigate how reactive-power compensation affects receiving-end voltage in a transmission system and compare the voltage-support characteristics of SVC and STATCOM.

Suggested Procedure

  1. Run the system without compensation.
  2. Increase QL and observe voltage depression.
  3. Apply SVC compensation.
  4. Replace SVC with STATCOM.
  5. Compare Q support and Vr.
  6. Increase X to represent a weak transmission corridor.

Reactive element Variable susceptance Converter voltage source
Shunt connection Yes Yes
Voltage control Fast Very fast

Virtual Laboratory Observation

The receiving-end voltage is strongly influenced by transmission reactance and reactive loading. Increasing QL or X increases the voltage drop across the transmission corridor.

An SVC changes its effective shunt susceptance to inject or absorb reactive power. Its reactive-power output is approximately proportional to Vr2.

A STATCOM regulates reactive current through a controllable converter voltage. The simulation therefore illustrates why STATCOM-based compensation can provide strong voltage support during depressed-voltage conditions.
